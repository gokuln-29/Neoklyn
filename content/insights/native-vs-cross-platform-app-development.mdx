Most founders ask "should I build native or cross-platform?" before they've answered the question that actually determines the answer: what does the app need to do, and what's the budget for doing it twice versus once?

Native vs cross-platform app development gets treated as a technology preference. It's really a budget and timeline decision wearing a technical costume.

## The Question Founders Ask Backwards

Native means writing separate codebases for iOS (Swift) and Android (Kotlin) — two apps, built twice, that happen to do the same thing. Cross-platform means writing one codebase in a framework like Flutter or React Native that compiles to both platforms.

The instinct is to ask which one is "better." That's the wrong first question, because both are genuinely good engineering choices — the right one depends entirely on what you're building and what happens after launch, not on which framework is trendier. Framed correctly, native vs cross-platform app development is really two separate questions stacked together: what does the app technically need, and what can the budget sustain over the next two years of updates, not just the first release.

## What Actually Differs

The real differences that matter for a business decision:

- **Codebase count.** Native is two codebases maintained in parallel forever. Cross-platform is one codebase, one team, one set of bugs to fix instead of two.
- **Access to platform-specific features.** Native has zero-lag access to the newest iOS/Android APIs the day they ship. Cross-platform frameworks usually catch up within weeks to months, which matters for apps that lean heavily on brand-new OS capabilities.
- **Performance ceiling.** Native has a slightly higher performance ceiling for graphics-heavy or hardware-intensive work (AR, complex animations, background processing). For the large majority of business apps — booking, ordering, content, CRM-style tools — the difference is not perceptible to users.
- **Long-term maintenance cost.** This is the one people underweight. Two codebases mean two sets of dependency updates, two sets of OS-version bugs, and two review cycles every time something changes.

## Cost: The Real Numbers

These are the bands we actually quote from, not a marketing range: mobile app projects typically run ₹1-4 lakh (~$1,200-$5,000) depending on scope, whether that's a cross-platform build on Flutter or React Native, or a native iOS/Android app. The number moves based on features and integrations, not which technology you pick in isolation — but two separate native codebases for the same feature set costs meaningfully more than one cross-platform codebase covering both, because you're paying for the work to be done twice.

If a native quote and a cross-platform quote for the *same* feature set come back within a few percent of each other, ask what's being cut — usually it's one platform getting less testing time than the other. ## Timeline: What to Actually Expect

Standard-scope mobile apps typically take 3-6 weeks; complex platforms with heavy integrations run 6-10 weeks. Cross-platform builds tend to land toward the faster end of that range because there's one codebase to build, test, and polish instead of two running in parallel.

One timeline factor both approaches share and founders consistently forget: app store review. [Apple's App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) cover a real approval process that can take anywhere from under a day to over a week, especially on a first submission or for apps handling payments, health data, or user-generated content. Neither native nor cross-platform skips this step — budget for it in your launch date, not as an afterthought after the build is "done."

## When Native Genuinely Wins

Native is the right call when:

- The app is built around brand-new OS capabilities that cross-platform frameworks haven't caught up to yet
- Performance is genuinely the product — high-end gaming, real-time AR, or heavy background processing where every millisecond matters
- You only need one platform (iOS-only or Android-only) and there's no realistic near-term plan to expand to the other

## When Cross-Platform Is the Right Call

Cross-platform is the right call for most business apps — the majority of what we build falls here — when:

- You need both iOS and Android from day one, and a single team maintaining one codebase is worth more than a marginal performance gain
- Time-to-market matters, because you validate on one build instead of two
- The app is UI-driven rather than hardware-driven: booking, ordering, content, dashboards, CRM tools, marketplaces

## The Mistake We See Most Often

The most expensive mistake isn't picking the "wrong" framework — both Flutter and React Native are production-proven, used by companies at serious scale. It's picking native for a straightforward business app because it sounds more premium, then discovering the maintenance bill is double for a performance benefit nobody actually notices.

The reverse mistake also happens: choosing cross-platform for a genuinely hardware-intensive app and hitting a performance ceiling months into development, after the budget and timeline were already set around the wrong assumption.

## How to Decide for Your Project

The decision comes down to two honest questions: does this app depend on hardware-level performance or day-one access to brand-new OS features, and does the budget support building and maintaining two codebases instead of one? If the answer to both is no — which is true for most business apps — cross-platform is very likely the right call, not a compromise.
